# Techne

## 1 / Open

### A web of peers {#open}

The internet started as a simple promise. Any point could reach any other point.

The rules that made this work were public. Nobody owned them. Email still works this way: you can write to anyone, whoever their provider is.

What grew on those rules was a web of peers. Each of us could speak, and each of us could answer.

#### Under the hood

The internet's base protocols (TCP/IP, email, the web) are open standards.

The early internet was open, but it was also small and far from equal. The claim here is about its shape, not a golden age.

The network drawing is inspired by Figure 1 of [Paul Baran’s 1964 *On Distributed Communications*](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_memoranda/RM3420.html), comparing centralized, decentralized and distributed networks.

### What a web of peers can do {#peers}

No one is in charge of a forest, a flock, or a conversation among friends. The intelligence is in the connections.

## 2 / Captured

### Something moves into the middle {#captured}

Then something offered to make connecting easier. Come inside, and everyone you know will be here.

It worked. And one by one, our lines to each other began to pass through the middle.

A big platform holds four things as one: your name, everything you have made, the way people find you, and the only window onto all of it.

### The flow turns one way {#flow}

In a web of peers, what you give goes to someone, and something comes back.

In the star, what you give goes to the middle. The middle decides what is seen, and by whom.

#### Under the hood

The amber beads in the illustration stand for the argument that value is gathered in the middle. They are not a financial measurement.

### The middle keeps growing {#acceleration}

When systems compete to hold more of our attention, more powerful tools can make that pull stronger.

More of our common life can come to depend on fewer places.

This illustration explores a risk, not an inevitable outcome.

#### Under the hood

Some writers call an accelerating competitive feedback loop of this kind "Moloch": everyone in the middle pulls harder because everyone else does. The lavender ribbon in the illustration is that feedback loop. It is not a creature, a machine with intentions, or a forecast.

The towers, the ribbon and the shadow over shared places are metaphors for incentives and concentrated power. They are not historical reconstruction or data.

### Why it holds {#leave-captured}

This is not a story about bad people. Every company in the middle faces the same pull.

You win by holding the most people and being the hardest to leave. So you hoard, and you make leaving costly. Then the only thing left to grow is how much of each person's attention you take. Everyone else in the middle is doing the same.

And we stay, because the people we care about are already there.

#### Under the hood

Experiments with college students found many active Instagram and TikTok users would prefer a world without the platform, yet would not leave alone (Bursztyn et al., [NBER working paper w31771](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31771)). Scope: college students, those two platforms.

The shape rewards capture, or tilts toward it. It does not fully explain it. Advertising incentives can survive on an open network too.

## 3 / Freed

### Take the middle back out {#freed}

Here is the change. Unglue the four things.

Your name and what you have made stay with you, in a home you choose.

Finding and viewing become services that anyone can offer, and you can pick among them.

#### Under the hood

This is what AT Protocol does. Your records and identity live with a host you choose (a PDS). Indexes and apps (AppViews) are built by anyone over the shared data. You can move hosts without the old host's permission, given that you control your identity and have a copy of your data.

"Peer to peer" in this story describes relationships between people and communities. AT Protocol itself is a federation of servers, and your phone is not a peer on the network. You carry records of your relationships; other people's attention stays theirs.

### Free to leave, so worth staying {#homes}

A host that cannot hold you has one way left to keep you: being worth staying for.

### Many small centers {#groups}

We are usually offered two choices. Do everything alone, or hand everything to a giant.

Life happens in between: the household, the co-op, the neighborhood.

Each group decides what it shares with the world, what it shares among its members, and what it keeps.

#### Under the hood

Subsidiarity and solidarity. [Atproto Spaces](https://atproto.com/blog/atproto-spaces-alpha) are in alpha and provide access control for group data. They do not give secrecy: access control is not encryption.

## 4 / Alive

### Giving that comes back {#alive}

In the star, every newcomer had to start from nothing and fight for a share.

In the web, a newcomer starts with everything already there, and adds to it.

An act of giving can begin a relationship where meaningful exchange becomes possible.

#### Under the hood

A gift that travels on is an illustration of reciprocity. It is not a promise that every gift is repaid.

A shared calendar is one possible use: compatible tools could read the same event records instead of maintaining separate copies.

### In service of the web of life {#place}

A captured network pulls value away from places and toward its center. An open one can let value stay where it is made: with a household, a neighborhood, a watershed.

Imagine neighbors sharing tools, tending gardens and finding one another through gatherings. Each small exchange strengthens the relationships that make a place worth belonging to.

The digital web becomes a means of caring for the living web around us. This is a possibility to work toward, not an outcome technology can promise.

#### Under the hood

The villages, gardens and groves are an imagined possibility. An open network does not automatically produce an ecological society, and nothing here claims that companies turn into villages. The picture shows functions being reclaimed and relationships reconnected.

### Still unfinished {#unfinished}

This is not finished, and it will not finish itself.

Three places the middle could grow back: the directory that says who everyone is, the service that helps people find each other, and the shared words everyone has to agree on.

#### Under the hood

The identity directory, network indexes and shared vocabularies each need accountable governance. [Independent PLC replicas](https://atproto.com/blog/plc-replicas) make it possible to audit directory operations; replication alone does not settle governance. These are places to keep shaping the protocol together.

### Techne {#techne}

The tools we shape also shape the lives we can lead.

The Greeks had a word for the knowing that lives in making a thing well: *techne*.

Three things a toolmaker has to learn. Know yourself. Know the system your tool will enter. Know how to build.

Techne is a community of practice: learning how to shape tools in service of living relationships.

Toward an integrated society: connected without being made the same.
